# Diving-mask intro: visual and scroll notes

The current detailed interactive preview is `shark-intro-mask-scroll-preview-v2.html`; v1 remains saved for comparison. It precedes the bright blacktip reef scene. It starts with a painted mask and the only introductory words, **“lets dive!”**; scrolling brings the mask toward the visitor, reveals the underwater reef through its lens, crosses the watercolor waterline, moves into the reef, and then reveals the blacktip shark. Reverse scrolling retraces the same movement. The text is deliberately a new exception to the earlier no-words-on-screen direction, as requested for the intro.

The preview uses a GSAP ScrollTrigger progress callback when its pinned CDN scripts are available, with a native scroll-position fallback so the local file still works without network access. The images are key art, and the mask movement is a prototype for the eventual frame-matched ScrollWorld sequence. The final seam from the mask to the blacktip will need the same visual continuity checks as the other handoffs.

The four intro image assets were created with the built-in imagegen tool. `shark-intro-watercolor-diving-mask-v1.png` is a transparent foreground cutout; `shark-intro-ocean-surface-v1.png` is the detailed above-water opening; `shark-intro-waterline-v1.png` bridges the dive; and `shark-intro-empty-reef-v1.png` is the shark-free ocean beneath the mask. The first blacktip image is the existing approved `shark-scrollworld-style-frame-v1.png`. The words are HTML text so they remain spelled exactly as requested and can fade cleanly during scrolling.

The v2 scroll path is deliberately staged: surface glints and a slight camera drift; mask approach with lens reflections and sliding water droplets; a momentary darkening around the mask as it meets the viewer; foamy surface crossing; rising bubbles; a calm look into the empty reef; then the blacktip reveal. The palette is sunlit cream `#fffef0`, sea-glass turquoise `#60cbd7`, deep teal mask `#064d63`, and cool blue shadow `#063b5b`. Georgia italic carries the single line of text; the illustrations carry everything else. The extra detail is concentrated in the physical act of entering the water, with no additional written captions.

**Diving-mask cutout** — style reference: `shark-scrollworld-style-frame-v1.png`.

> Use case: illustration-story. Asset type: isolated transparent-background foreground cutout for a scroll-driven underwater story intro. Style reference: supplied storybook watercolor reef and blacktip shark frame; match its expressive hand-painted pigment granulation, soft feathered paint edges, sea-glass turquoise, muted teal, and gentle ink details. Create a SINGLE large front-facing vintage DIVING MASK, centered and almost filling a wide 16:9 canvas, viewed as if the visitor is about to put it on. Use one broad continuous rounded-rectangle viewing window and a dark muted teal rubber rim with painterly highlights; short strap ends may extend toward left and right edges. The central lens/window must be genuinely TRANSPARENT ALPHA so a separate ocean image can be seen clearly through it, and the entire region outside the mask must also be transparent alpha. Only the rim, bridge details, a few subtle glass highlights along the edge and strap are painted. Keep all details within the frame with generous safe margins. No face, eyes, person, ocean background, fish, shark, bubbles, text, logo, border, photorealism, or opaque filled lens.
